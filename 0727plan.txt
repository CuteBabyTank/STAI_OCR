# Snag Capstone Evaluation — Complete Context and Repository-Audit Request

I need you to take over the evaluation work for our Snag capstone. You have access to the current codebase.

## 1. Project overview

Snag is an integrated receipt-processing and personal-finance platform built for our STAI capstone.

The documented system includes:

### Receipt-processing capabilities

* Image, batch, and PDF receipt processing
* Receipt OCR and structured extraction
* Header fields such as merchant, TIN, address, receipt number, date, tax, payment information, and total
* Line-item extraction
* Deterministic cleanup and payment-field reconciliation
* Receipt arithmetic reconciliation
* Confidence and review/disambiguation flags
* Receipt and line-item persistence
* Receipt embeddings and semantic search
* Receipt-to-finance posting

### Agent-related capabilities

* Read-only natural-language-to-SQL questions
* Receipt-grounded RAG questions
* Receipt citations
* ReAct-style routing between SQL and receipt search
* Pre-loop disambiguation
* Maximum-step and repeated-tool-call guards
* MLflow traces for some OCR, SQL, RAG, or ReAct operations

### Personal-finance capabilities

* Accounts and balances
* Expense, income, and transfer transactions
* Rule-based Quick Chat transaction drafts
* Categories, tags, and templates
* Budgets
* Recurring transactions and installments
* Goals, debts, and receivables
* History and statistics
* JSON backup and restore

These are documented capabilities, not automatically verified evaluation results.

## 2. Important factual constraints

Preserve the following cautions throughout the audit:

1. Do not assume Snag uses LangGraph.
2. Do not assume Snag consists of multiple independent agents.
3. Do not introduce LangGraph solely to imitate lecture examples.
4. The current system reportedly has no user-account/authentication layer.
5. Do not add authentication requirements solely for evaluation.
6. Quick Chat is a deterministic rule-based parser, not an LLM feature.
7. SQL transaction execution or instructions such as “transfer ₱10,000 through an agent” are out of scope.
8. Read-only SQL questions may remain in scope.
9. The submitted Docker configuration reportedly points to a shared remote Ollama endpoint by default.
10. Do not describe the default deployment as fully offline or on-device without verifying the actual configuration.
11. JSON backup and restore is not equivalent to authenticated cloud synchronization.
12. Historical development-model accuracy must not be attributed to the current deployment.
13. “No API fee” does not mean zero hosting, compute, electricity, maintenance, or correction cost.
14. Receipt-level exact match and field-level accuracy are different metrics.
15. The fact that every tested receipt contained at least one error would imply 0% receipt-level exact match for that set, not necessarily 0% field-level accuracy.
16. Do not invent sample sizes, repetitions, thresholds, savings, accuracy, or customer outcomes.
17. Any new sample size or acceptance threshold must be labelled as proposed by the team.
18. Do not erase failed, slow, or incomplete test cases from the results.

## 5. Separate project-specific consultation

A separate consultation established Snag’s project-specific proof point.

The professor advised the team to:

* Hone in on the intelligent use case
* Avoid becoming distracted by ordinary finance-app features
* Focus more on experiment design than whether Snag wins
* Accept either a positive or negative result
* Use ground truth and clearly explain experiment learnings
* Include a retrospective
* Compare against a strong benchmark rather than weaker products
* Primarily compare against Claude Cowork
* Optionally include a web-based LLM baseline
* Keep Tarsi as Philippine market context
* Keep SQL execution of financial transactions out of scope

The proposed main personal use case was:

> Cross-check receipts against bank and credit-card statements.

The proposed secondary business use case was:

> Compare an encoded expense sheet against a folder of receipts.

Major concerns included:

* Adding bank and credit-card statements to accounts
* Ensuring OCR does not hallucinate receipt totals

The intended comparison is therefore approximately:

> How reliably can Snag extract, verify, and reconcile receipt-based financial information, and how does it compare with Claude Cowork on the same finance-verification task?

Do not phrase the research question as assuming Snag will outperform Cowork.
